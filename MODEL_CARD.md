## 2. Intended use

**Primary intended use.** Code and data to use LLM predictions as stratification signals for sampling-based KG accuracy estimation. Facts are partitioned into strata according to aggregated predictions of several LLMs, and accuracy is estimated from human labels collected within the strata. For large KGs, student classifiers trained on LLM pseudo-labels for a sample of facts produce the signals at lower cost.

**Intended users.** Researchers and developers working on data quality and KG evaluation.

**Out-of-scope uses.** This release must not be used for
- clinical diagnosis, prognosis or treatment of individual patients;
- decisions about individuals, such as insurance, employment or access to care;
- re-identification of individuals or linkage with other data sources;
- populations, devices or data types not described in Section 3;
- use of LLM or student-model predictions as direct accuracy estimates or as ground truth for single facts.

## 4. Method

- **Algorithm or architecture.** Four LLMs accessed through Azure AI Inference (Cohere Command R 08-2024, DeepSeek-V3-0324, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Mistral Nemo) label KG facts; their aggregated predictions define the strata used for sampling-based accuracy estimation. Student models are BERT classifiers (`bert-base-uncased`, sequence classification) fine-tuned on LLM pseudo-labels.
- **Training setting.** Centralized.
- **Privacy techniques.** None; only public data are used.

## 5. Performance

- **Evaluation data.** Real-world KGs (NELL, DBpedia, FactBench) and three noisy variants of YAGO 4.5 with accuracy 0.75, 0.50 and 0.25.
- **Metrics and results.** On real-world KGs, stratification with aggregated LLM predictions reduces annotation cost by 11% to 54% with respect to unstratified and topology-based baselines, with statistical guarantees on the estimates. Used as direct estimators, LLMs deviate by up to 45% from the true accuracy. Student models are trained on LLM labels for 0.25% of the facts. Detailed results are in the paper and in `/extra-experiments`.
- **Results by subgroup.** Not applicable (no human population); results per dataset are reported in the paper.

## 6. Known limitations and risks

- **Generalizability.** Gains depend on how well LLM predictions correlate with fact correctness on the target KG.
- **Known biases and confounders.** LLMs show systematic biases and knowledge gaps, which affect the quality of the strata but not the unbiasedness of the estimates, since these rely on human labels.
- **Failure modes.** Uninformative LLM predictions yield little or no gain over unstratified sampling. Running the LLMs requires Azure credentials and endpoints configured by the user.
- **Privacy risks.** Student models are trained on public data and their weights are not released; membership inference and model inversion tests do not apply.
